# Canadian buyer's layer fills the app's biggest gaps

The LAZ-Boy app is already strong on product specs, so the highest-value additions are not new chairs. They are the Canadian purchase layer (lift-chair tax rules, financing, delivery, protection plan, sit-test language), care and service procedures, and a few record-level gaps. The model gap against the US site is small: of 102 upholstery model names in the US product sitemap, 96 are already in the app ([sitemap_detail0.xml](https://www.la-z-boy.com/sitemap_detail0.xml)). The real record gaps are Mercury, Addison, Gibson, Coleman, Casey, Hudson, Rigby, Emric, Elliot and Lakewood. The PDF is the opposite case: it holds **329 available product codes, 993 covers and 301 coming-soon codes**, yet only **47 products and 60 covers** match app data, and 33 of 301 coming-soon codes are in the style index (`pdf_vs_app.md`). The best Canadian additions come from one Ottawa/Gatineau/Kingston dealer group, not corporate La-Z-Boy, so they must be tagged dealer-specific and store-configurable. The `la-z-boy.ca` pages could not be loaded (TLS error), so no Canada-wide terms were verified. Several web claims contradict app data and should not be imported (section 4). The top five actions are: add the lift-chair tax and prescription card, add the PDF product and cover codes (after confirming that list's market), backfill 398 blank swatch names, add the care and iClean procedures, and add the sit-test and fit language.

## 2. What each source adds

### available_products.pdf: the largest unmatched dataset

The parsed PDF contains **329 unique product codes** in prefix-model form (for example 44P-726 maps to app styleNumber 44P726), **993 cover codes** (for example B156175 is cover B1561 plus color 75) and **301 coming-soon codes** labelled "Coming Soon Styles (Oct 18)". Only 47 of the 329 product codes exist in `style-index-2026.json`, 60 of 993 covers exist in `swatch-manifest.json`, only 19 of 269 five-character cover patterns appear in `fabric-cover-program-a26.json`, and 33 of the 301 coming-soon codes are in the style index (`pdf_vs_app.md`). The 58 absent style prefixes and about 80 prefixes missing from motion-catalog definitions mean the app's style coverage is much narrower than the PDF's, while model-name coverage is much better (96 of 102 US sitemap names present). Counting from the parsed file, the products list spans 90 distinct three-character prefixes (`_pdf_codes.json`). The parsed codes are in `_pdf_codes.json` (keys `products`, `covers`, `soon`). The notes do not say whether this PDF is a Canadian or US availability list, and that decides how it should be imported.

### stylemeetscomfort.ca: best-selling lift chairs article and lift content

The app already carries all four lift tiers (Bronze 3BL 300 lb, Silver 4LP 325 lb, Gold 1HM 500 lb, Platinum 1PL/1PH 350 lb), and its Clayton, Jean and Billie dimensions match the dealer's exactly ([Lift Chair Guide](https://www.stylemeetscomfort.ca/learning-center/lift-chairs-guide), [Clayton](https://www.stylemeetscomfort.ca/learning-center/la-z-boy-clayton-lift-recliner-review)). What is new is the Canadian purchasing layer. The tax rules are the largest item ([Tax article](https://www.stylemeetscomfort.ca/learning-center/lift-chairs-tax-deductible-canada)): the dealer's worked example says a **$3,000 chair with delivery and protection could save up to $390 in Ontario** at 13% HST ([Cost article](https://www.stylemeetscomfort.ca/learning-center/lift-recliner-chair-cost)). Other new items are Flexiti financing, white-glove delivery at $129.99 (Ottawa, Gatineau, Kingston) or $169.99 out of town, per-model height guidance, and cover counts per lift. The dealer's per-model starting prices are Billie $1,639, Jean $1,099, Liam $1,879, Pinnacle $2,929, Astor $3,539 and Clayton $3,869 (the Clayton store listing shows $4,389.95 instead), each dated in its own article. Lift chairs do not come in real leather, per the guide. One name needs caution: a "Miller" lift appears only as a photo filename (`S80i_MILLER_2BL-792`), with no text and no app record, so do not add it. The James lift (Silver, 4LP521) has dealer dimensions (D 39.5, H 43.5, W 40 in; seat height 22 in), but the mapping to 4LP is an inference ([James review](https://www.stylemeetscomfort.ca/learning-center/review-james-furniture-family)).

### stylemeetscomfort.ca: recliners buyer's guide and the wider learning center

The learning center holds **370 articles** across 37 index pages (pages 38 and 40 return 404), but only about 21 were read in full, so most of it is unread ([index notes](https://www.stylemeetscomfort.ca/learning-center)). The buyer's guide supplies sit-test and discovery scripts, a household and room-function decision framework, a manual-vs-power table, and a 7-feature "La-Z-Boy Only" talk track ([guide](https://www.stylemeetscomfort.ca/learning-center/recliners-buyers-guide), [7 features](https://www.stylemeetscomfort.ca/learning-center/7-exclusive-features-la-z-boy-furniture)). Custom-order lead times are 6-10 weeks for a custom recliner, 8-12 for a sofa and 8-14 for a sectional ([lead times](https://www.stylemeetscomfort.ca/learning-center/how-long-custom-furniture-build)). The Summit Series review adds tall enhancements (wider frame, extra springs, heavy-duty mechanism, taller base, longer handle) and dimensions: Randell 45 x 46.5 x 41.5 in and Redwood 46 x 49.5 x 45 in ([Summit](https://www.stylemeetscomfort.ca/learning-center/review-summit-series)). Other read articles add fabric-grade and leather education, iClean ($300-$600 chair upgrade) and pet-friendly fabric terms ([iClean](https://www.stylemeetscomfort.ca/learning-center/la-z-boy-iclean-review), [pet-friendly](https://www.stylemeetscomfort.ca/learning-center/pet-friendly-fabric-review)), power-control resync steps ([controls](https://www.stylemeetscomfort.ca/learning-center/power-control-instructions)), the rocker/glider/wall/swivel logic, and model reviews for Acadia, Quinault, Armitage, Trouper, Collins and Duo. Service pages add Flexiti details including Quebec rules, a 5-Year Protection Plan price ladder ($59 up to $549 for furniture up to $6,000, then $699 to $8,000, then 9% or 10% above) ([plan](https://www.stylemeetscomfort.ca/learning-center/furniture-protection-plan)), a five-store directory and a seniors/accessibility script.

## 3. Canada vs US and dealer vs corporate: how to label what you import

Three provenance classes appear in the notes, and each needs its own tag.

**Corporate, US-served.** The Customer Care, warranty, care, FAQ, manuals and swatch pages come from la-z-boy.com with a US country flag. All US pricing, the Veterans Day 35%-off promotion, "financing only applicable in the United States" text, the Synchrony card, the 5-year Easy Care plan, the US return policy and the Medicare NSC statement are US-only. The US return policy (3-day exchange, up to 30% restocking) appears word for word in an excerpt of the Canadian site, which suggests `.ca` reuses US copy ([return policy](https://www.la-z-boy.ca/content/policies/return-policy)).

**Corporate, Canadian, unreadable.** `www.la-z-boy.ca` is live and indexed, but every direct load failed with a certificate error, and the crawler did not bypass it. Only search excerpts exist. The Canadian Parts & Warranty excerpt repeats the US one-year upholstery/foam language and the same (855) 802-6636 number ([parts-warranty](https://www.la-z-boy.ca/content/CustomerCare/parts-warranty)). The `.ca` site confirms Collage, Harbor Town, Allegra, Brooks, Paxton and Collins ([Harbor Town](https://www.la-z-boy.ca/p/recliners/harbor-town-rocking-recliner/_/R-010799)). The `.ca` financing page names Synchrony Bank, a US issuer, so it may be stale ([financing-ca](https://www.la-z-boy.ca/financing-ca)). The notes therefore cannot settle whether Canadian warranty terms differ from the US table or from the app matrix.

**Dealer-specific, Canadian.** Everything on stylemeetscomfort.ca is one independent Ottawa, Gatineau and Kingston group (Cyrville, Hunt Club, Kanata, Kingston, Gatineau). Delivery fees, removal fees, the 12-week custom lead time, 50% deposit, 20% restocking fee, Flexiti plans, the protection plan and every "starting at" price belong to this group. They are not a La-Z-Boy Canada price list and may differ at other Canadian dealers. Pages 34-37 of the index have slug-derived titles only.

The practical rule is to add a provenance field (corporate-US, corporate-CA, dealer) and a store-config layer. Dealer fees and prices go in the store layer, never in global reference data. Every dealer price carries its as-of date.

## Conclusion

The crawl changes the shape of the work. The app does not lack products so much as it lacks provenance and a Canadian purchase layer, and the PDF shows the style-code gap is far larger than the model-name gap. The sensible build order is to import structured, high-confidence data first (swatch names, PDF codes after market confirmation, manuals links), then add procedural content (care, service, fit) with a US or dealer tag, and keep dealer fees and prices in a store-configurable module.

The conflicts list is as valuable as the additions. Most dealer disagreements involve the same few claims (heat and massage, battery packs on lifts, lift warranty, clearances), and each could cause a wrong statement on the floor if copied. The most useful single step is a Canadian corporate source, because the unresolved warranty and financing questions all point to one unreadable site.
